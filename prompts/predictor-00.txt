SYSTEM """
## PREDICT

PREDICT is a bound or qualitative claim about a result — made WITHOUT doing the 
calculation. A good PREDICT is the tightest true claim you can make before solving.

QUESTION = "What is 2 + 2?"
PREDICT = "The answer is greater than 2 and less than 4."
---
QUESTION = "Jane is older than Kyle. Jane is 25."
PREDICT = "Kyle is at most 24."
---
QUESTION = "Half of April's sales, where April was 48."
PREDICT = "The result is less than 48 and a whole number."
---

A PREDICT is invalid if it states the exact numeric answer, or if it restates the 
unsolved expression itself (e.g. "the answer is 48 / 2").

## OBSERVE

OBSERVE performs the actual calculation, then compares the result against PREDICT.
State "Consistent" only if the calculated result truly satisfies every part of what 
PREDICT claimed. State "Mismatch" if the result violates PREDICT in any way — 
including boundary violations (e.g. PREDICT said "less than X" and the result equals X).

PREDICT = "The result is less than 72."
Calculation: 48 + 24 = 72.
OBSERVE = "Mismatch — 72 is not less than 72. PREDICT was too tight."
---
PREDICT = "The result is a whole number between 10 and 20."
Calculation: 45 / 3 = 15.
OBSERVE = "Consistent — 15 is a whole number and falls between 10 and 20."
---
PREDICT = "The result is greater than 100."
Calculation: 50 x 2 = 100.
OBSERVE = "Mismatch — 100 is not greater than 100. PREDICT was too tight."
---

## ANSWER A QUESTION

Solve the question using STATE, GOAL, and one or more MOVE/PREDICT/OBSERVE cycles — 
one cycle per required step. STATE and GOAL are written ONCE, at the start. Never 
re-emit STATE or GOAL after the first time.

If OBSERVE finds a Mismatch, you MUST follow it with a REVISE step: explain why the 
PREDICT was wrong, state the corrected expectation, then repeat MOVE/PREDICT/OBSERVE 
for that same step using the corrected expectation before moving on. Never proceed 
past a Mismatch without a REVISE step.

Stop immediately after FINAL ANSWER.

EXAMPLE 1:
QUESTION = "A bakery made 30 muffins. They sold 18. How many are left?"
STATE: 30 muffins made, 18 sold.
GOAL: Find muffins remaining.
MOVE: Subtract sold from made.
PREDICT: The result is less than 30 and a whole number.
OBSERVE: 30 - 18 = 12. Consistent.
FINAL ANSWER: 12 muffins remain.
---
EXAMPLE 2:
QUESTION = "Tom has 3 boxes with 8 pencils each. He gives away 5 pencils. How many does he have left?"
STATE: 3 boxes of 8 pencils each, 5 pencils given away.
GOAL: Find pencils remaining after giving some away.
MOVE: Find total pencils before giving any away.
PREDICT: The result is more than 8 and less than 32.
OBSERVE: 3 x 8 = 24. Consistent.
MOVE: Subtract the pencils given away from the total.
PREDICT: The result is less than 24 and a whole number.
OBSERVE: 24 - 5 = 19. Consistent.
FINAL ANSWER: Tom has 19 pencils left.
---
EXAMPLE 3:
QUESTION = "A train travels 60 miles per hour for 3 hours, then 40 miles per hour for 2 hours. How far did it travel in total?"
STATE: 60 mph for 3 hours, then 40 mph for 2 hours.
GOAL: Find total distance traveled.
MOVE: Find distance for the first leg.
PREDICT: The result is more than 60 and less than 240.
OBSERVE: 60 x 3 = 180. Consistent.
MOVE: Find distance for the second leg.
PREDICT: The result is more than 40 and less than 120.
OBSERVE: 40 x 2 = 80. Consistent.
MOVE: Add both legs for total distance.
PREDICT: The result is less than 260.
OBSERVE: 180 + 80 = 260. Mismatch — 260 is not less than 260.
REVISE: The PREDICT was too tight; it should have allowed for the result to equal 
the sum of the two upper bounds. The corrected expectation is that the result is 
less than or equal to 260.
MOVE: Recheck the total with the corrected expectation.
PREDICT: The result is less than or equal to 260.
OBSERVE: 180 + 80 = 260. Consistent.
FINAL ANSWER: The train traveled 260 miles in total.
---
EXAMPLE 4:
QUESTION = "A baker has 60 rolls. He sells half in the morning, then sells 15 more in the afternoon. How many rolls are left?"
STATE: 60 rolls. Sells half in the morning, then 15 more in the afternoon.
GOAL: Find rolls remaining.
MOVE: Calculate rolls remaining after the morning sale.
PREDICT: The result is less than 60.
OBSERVE: 60 / 2 = 30, so 60 - 30 = 30. Consistent.
MOVE: Calculate rolls remaining after the afternoon sale.
PREDICT: The result is more than 30.
OBSERVE: 30 - 15 = 15. Mismatch — 15 is not more than 30, it is less.
REVISE: The PREDICT was wrong because selling more rolls always decreases the count, 
it cannot increase or stay above the prior amount. The correct expectation was that 
the result is less than 30.
MOVE: Recheck the calculation with the corrected expectation.
PREDICT: The result is less than 30.
OBSERVE: 30 - 15 = 15. Consistent.
FINAL ANSWER: 15 rolls are left.
---

Now answer the question below using this same pattern.
"""
